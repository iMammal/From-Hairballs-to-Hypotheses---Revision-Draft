# Proposed manuscript text

## End of Section 3 introduction paragraph

Figure~\ref{fig:assistance-modality-map} organizes representative assessed systems by the assistance mechanism they implement and the visualization environment in which that mechanism operates. Analytical tasks cut across the matrix: the same system may support navigation, comparison, precision interaction, hypothesis development, or collaborative reasoning, and a system may occupy more than one cell when the full report supports each assistance-modality relationship. Read through the lens of analytic agency, the examples differ in who initiates and specifies an operation, what the system executes, and how the analyst interprets or corrects the result. The map is illustrative rather than enumerative; it does not estimate prevalence in the literature.

## Publication-ready caption

**Assistance × visualization modality in the assessed examples.** Rows show the four retained, overlapping assistance modes and columns show the five retained visualization modalities. Task symbols identify the analytical intents supported by each illustrated system: N, Navigation and Multiscale Orientation; C, Comparison and Differentiation; F, Selection, Filtering, and Precision Interaction; H, Sensemaking and Hypothesis Development; and R, Coordination and Collaborative Reasoning. A system appears in multiple cells only when its full report supports the corresponding assistance-modality mechanism; display type, adjustable parameters, or speech input alone do not create an additional placement. The dashed boundary box identifies contextual or unresolved evidence and is not part of the eligible-example matrix. Empty cells mean that the combination is not represented in these assessed examples, not that no such systems or literature exist. The figure encodes no counts or prevalence.

## Suggested Section 6 cross-references

At the start of the evaluation synthesis, after introducing cross-cutting evaluation dimensions, add:

> The placements in Figure~\ref{fig:assistance-modality-map} are claims about implemented assistance-modality mechanisms, not comparative effectiveness. Evaluation must therefore ask separately whether a mechanism improves analytic outcomes, preserves representational fidelity, supports correction and calibration, reduces interaction cost, or strengthens collaborative common ground.

When discussing heterogeneous evidence, add:

> The matrix also makes the coverage imbalance visible without converting it into a frequency claim: the assessed examples populate several Desktop/Planar and VR cells, whereas other combinations remain unrepresented or unresolved. These gaps motivate targeted evidence collection but cannot support prevalence estimates from the provisional candidate labels.
