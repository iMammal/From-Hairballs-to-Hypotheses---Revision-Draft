\section{Deduplication and Related Reports}
Deduplication consolidated 187,446 source occurrences into 140,959 bibliographic records. A \emph{source occurrence} is an entry returned by a provider or imported from another source; several occurrences may refer to the same publication. A \emph{canonical record} is the representative bibliographic entry retained after applying the identity rules.

Matching used normalized DOI keys where available and exact normalized-title keys otherwise. Records with and without DOIs were kept separate when title agreement alone did not establish their identity. arXiv identity adjudications were incorporated into the final total. Source occurrences and identity decisions were retained so that canonical records could be traced to their acquisition routes.

Fifty related-version links connect publications such as preprints and published articles. These links preserve relationships between reports that remain separate bibliographic records. Grouping reports about the same software system occurs during full-text assessment, as described in the main paper.


\subsection{Retrieval and Screening Accounting}
The retrieval cutoff was 20 September 2026, without a lower publication-date limit. The local arXiv snapshot contained 3,164,528 records and yielded 958 distinct query-family matches; observed metadata updates extended through 11 September 2026. Its coverage is distinct from the cutoff for the other retrieval routes. Ehlers-associated working materials, Joos-associated companion material, and the reconstruction of Fonnet and Pri\'e's bibliography contributed 816, 146, and 207 source occurrences, respectively. These entered the same screening workflow as database results; source inventories distinguish confirmed survey members, unconfirmed entries, and background references.

Of 140,959 deduplicated records, 140,810 received valid screening outcomes: 9,505 Advance, 83,258 Defer, and 48,047 Excluded. The other 149 comprised 110 reserved pilot/calibration records, 35 technical failures, and four ambiguous requests that were not resubmitted. These states are not scientific exclusions. Of the excluded records, 10,460 also carried a background-literature flag indicating potential contextual or discovery value; these flags are a subset of exclusions, not additional records.


\section{Provisional Candidate Classification}
The task groups, assistance modes, and visualization modalities are defined in Section 3 of the main paper. Each label was assigned a Present, Absent, or Uncertain state, and multiple labels could coexist. Classification used evidence for the specific category: for example, unspecified display information yielded uncertainty about modality. The same category definitions were applied to all 9,505 advanced records using their supplied titles and abstracts.

These provisional classifications organized the candidate pool and guided selection for full-text assessment. Table~\ref{tab:candidate-census} summarizes assistance--modality co-occurrences among candidate records. A record contributes to a cell when both labels are Present; cells overlap. In the export, Present and Uncertain labels are listed explicitly, with omission denoting Absent under the classification contract. Desktop/Planar, for example, was Present in 2,912 records and Uncertain in 4,072.

The table describes labels supported by bibliographic metadata. Establishing that a particular assistance mechanism operates within a particular display environment requires assessment of the system description, which underlies Figure 3 of the main paper.

\subsection{Classification Contract and Interpretation}
Present denotes positive title/abstract evidence for a label; Absent denotes a negative category assignment under the coding contract; Uncertain records insufficient or ambiguous evidence. Absent is therefore not a full-report finding that a capability is missing. Assistance, modality, and task labels may coexist. Their definitions are those in Section 3 of the main paper, including substantive computation for Algorithmic assistance, a signal-linked computational intervention for Adaptive assistance, interpreted language for Conversational assistance, and computational use of spatial or embodied context for Immersive assistance. Display size or a headset alone does not supply the latter evidence.

The candidate table counts records with both assistance and modality marked Present, without asserting that the mechanism operates in that environment. Framework placements instead require report-level evidence for that relationship. Historical export fields and labels remain unchanged; the manuscript's eligibility criteria are E1--E5. The archived prompts, protocols, and screening settings preserve the execution specifications rather than a retrospective reconstruction of them.

\section{Purpose-Specific Selection Rules}
The 384 selected report identities represent six sets and 381 systems, not 384 completed assessments or one uniform sample. Of the report identities, 376 link exactly to the terminal 9,505-record pool. Three additional systems entered through coauthor-supplied reports and five through bounded early map expansion. A distinct prior-survey-only full-report selection route is not established by the cumulative crosswalk.

\subsection{Initial 20 entries}
Purposive anchors from the lead author's prior reading and coauthor-supplied reports were combined with assessed coauthor reports to establish the mechanism map. The candidate export resolved identities of already chosen examples, rather than ranking them. The lead author supplied the twelve anchor nominations; a Codex agent nominated the replacement and seven additions from coauthor-source evidence. The initial 20 was a resulting set, not a documented numerical cap. The twelve anchors were not ordered or capped in advance; no formal tie-breaking rule was recorded for this purposive set.

\subsection{Bounded 18 entries}
A frozen queue excluded previously represented systems and prioritized reusable lawful local reports, machine-coded candidates for empty cells, sparse Large Display/AR/MR/CAVE cases with explicit mechanism leads, and bounded contrast cases. Per-entry rationales and queue order are retained. The lead author specified the priorities and a maximum of 40; a Codex agent authored the exact 18-entry queue. The resulting queue contained 18 nominations against those priorities within the 40-entry bound; it was not a fixed-size sample. Within-priority tie rules were not recorded.

\subsection{Expanded 50 entries}
Software ranked candidates after excluding prior identities. Scores reflected rare modalities, non-desktop Adaptive/Conversational labels, mechanism/evaluation terms, contrast terms, and access indicators; ordering used descending score then lowercase title. The final 50 were a separately specified list with per-entry rationales, not the ranked top 50. Software validated and materialized that list within a maximum of 60. A Codex agent curated the final nominations under the lead author's maximum of 60. The resulting list contained 50 nominations against the stated priorities within the 60-entry bound; it was not a fixed-size sample or the top 50 of the ranked export. Selection among similarly suitable records was not governed by a recorded tie-breaking rule.

\subsection{Deterministic 200 entries}
Software validated the 9,505-record input and excluded previously considered canonical IDs, exact titles and DOIs, and subsequent exact DOI/title duplicates. Scores combined sparse-cell coverage, workflow, life-science, interaction, computation, evaluation/mechanism terms, abstracts, likely open access, assistance/modality bonuses, and contrast indicators. The implemented ordering was high-precision signal, rationale class, likely-open-access status, descending score, then canonical identifier. The lead author authorized the 200-entry bound and limit of 140 completed new assessments. The first 200 were frozen: 1--140 were primary and 141--200 ordered reserves. Retrieval continued through queue exhaustion; the maximum of 140 concerned completed new assessments rather than selected report identities. Only 60 of the 200 report entries had accessible full text at the September cutoff; grouping related reports yielded 57 completed system assessments. Access, rather than the assessment ceiling, therefore limited completion. The original audit JSON's textual sort description omits the high-precision and open-access strata. A dated correction overlay, \path{queue_selection_audit_correction_20261009.json} in the same staging directory, records the implemented order and binds it to the executable script. The original audit remains unchanged; no selection was rerun and no queue membership, order, or assessment changed.

\subsection{Sparse-cell 24 entries}
An upstream 25-entry shortlist from an 8,832-record partial export allowed up to three candidates per sparse cell, favored abstracts and DOIs, and excluded prior exact IDs/titles. Software rebound the entries to the terminal 9,505 export and checked canonical IDs, DOI, normalized title, and related versions. Removing AREA 2013 as a related report of previously considered AREA 2012 yielded 24 entries in preserved priority order, within a maximum of 30. The lead author supplied the provisional 25-entry list and authorized the maximum of 30; software performed reconciliation. The supplied shortlist, rather than a fresh software ranking, determined membership; reconciliation determined the final count of 24 within the authorized bound. No formal within-cell tie-breaking rule was recorded.

\subsection{Complete target-cell pass: 72 new entries}
Software enumerated every exact Present-labelled record in Immersive $\times$ Desktop/Planar (112 memberships), Immersive $\times$ Large Display (20), and Adaptive $\times$ Large Display (12). The 144 memberships represented 131 records, including 58 prior exact identities. All entered processing; priority band, descending metadata score, and canonical identifier determined access order only. Identity reconciliation reduced them to 129 groups. Excluding already represented systems and related reports yielded 72 new systems in the cumulative selected set. The lead author specified and authorized the three target cells. The 72 new entries were the reconciliation outcome, not an inclusion quota.

Wave-specific scripts, frozen queues, source bindings, retrieval manifests, and assessment ledgers provide supporting trace; the retained record contains no dedicated execution logs for the static queues.

We confirmed these roles against the retained session logs on 9 October 2026 (\path{docs/full_report_selection_authority.md}). That record, linked from \path{docs/reproducibility.md}, distinguishes contemporaneous task evidence from retrospective author confirmation.

Nomination authority, automated assessment, later author review, and independent validation are distinct. We did not retain documentation of separate author approval of the exact constructed queues before assessment. The lead author reviewed and confirmed the Figure 3 placements. We did not retain dated row-level approval records, and this review does not constitute independent double coding or pool-wide screening validation. The crosswalk does not provide a reason for every other candidate's non-selection.

\section{Language Coverage and Evidence Limits}
The initial system assessment used accessible English-language reports. Language was not a provider-query or title-and-abstract screening gate. A subsequent audit distinguished provider-reported language, title/abstract language signals, and full-report language; an English abstract was not treated as proof of an English report. The 9,505-record candidate export contains no language column.

A bounded follow-up considered seven reports from a 147-report lead set, with Trellet's French thesis considered separately. These reports have no finalized author-adjudicated eligibility and report--system reconciliation in the retained record. They therefore remain outside the approved system and placement counts. Their consideration does not establish exhaustive non-English coverage or screening reliability. Consequential translated passages require verification before any future inclusion. No provisional eligibility recommendation is presented here as an approved finding.

\section{Task-Symbol Interpretation}
Task letters identify navigation (N), selection/filtering (F), comparison (C), sensemaking (S), and coordination/collaborative reasoning (R). Coding follows descriptions of the implemented analytical workflow, not a demonstrated benefit or an interface affordance alone. Coordination can involve task assignment, handoff, or collective interpretation; it need not imply synchronous multiuser operation. Mechanism--environment placement evidence and task support are distinct. Retained placement rows include analytical-task descriptions, source locators, and qualifications. Repeated task letters do not establish equivalent support, performance, or evaluation across systems.

S identifiers link the compact and extended tables to \path{METHODS_PER_SYSTEM_EVIDENCE.csv}; its locator fields identify retained evidence passages and assessment files. Evaluation types summarize retained evidence, not study quality or demonstrated benefit. Computational includes technical benchmarks and model, phantom, or output validation. Expert cases include domain-expert feedback and scientific case analyses; controlled study requires a reported comparison with human participants. Deployment requires evaluation during sustained operational use. Demonstration only denotes illustrative examples without a structured evaluation established in the retained account; not established denotes insufficient attributable evaluation evidence. Types may coexist; counts overlap and cannot be summed as a partition.
