\section{Review Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}
The review combines broad candidate identification with staged screening and a focused synthesis based on examination of selected papers in full. It separates scientific eligibility, administrative verification, and descriptive coding. Retrieval breadth supports discovery; it does not establish completeness of the final synthesis. In particular, model-nominated candidates are not reported as a human-validated eligible corpus. The protocol evolved through recorded amendments: v2.0.0 deferred administrative verification, v2.1.0 introduced operational deferral, and v2.2.0 favored explicit analytical evidence for advancement.

\subsection{Review Scope and Eligibility}
\label{sec:methodology-scope}
The screening unit is a bibliographic record: a database entry describing a research publication through its title, available abstract, authors, identifiers, and other metadata. A record may lack an abstract. We distinguish these entries from the papers examined in full and from the software systems described in those papers. At least one identified system within a paper must jointly satisfy five scientific criteria; evidence from unrelated systems cannot be combined to qualify a record.
\begin{description}
\item[E1: Life-science application.] The system supports analysis, understanding, monitoring, decision-making, discovery, or scientific practice in a life-science or closely related domain. Training, teleoperation, or presentation alone does not establish analytical use.
\item[E2: Relational or multiscale relevance.] Analysis concerns explicit networks or relationships derived from spatial, temporal, multivariate, image, lineage, similarity, or other multiscale data. A node--link diagram is not required; a network term or image alone is insufficient.
\item[E3: Visual-analytics component.] A human uses an interactive or analytically meaningful visual representation to inspect, validate, steer, compare, or interpret data or computational results. Illustration or communication alone is insufficient.
\item[E4: Substantive computational assistance.] A mechanism contributes nontrivially within that analytical workflow, for example through clustering, layout, aggregation, annotation, ranking, prediction, or interpreted analytic operations. Ordinary rendering, pan/zoom, direct parameter entry, or database lookup alone does not qualify.
\item[E5: Human analytic relationship.] People meaningfully inspect, direct, validate, interpret, supervise, correct, or make decisions from the assisted process. Manual control of every operation is not required.
\end{description}

Administrative scope (E6) requires an eligible research report, English-language full text for assessment, and availability by the documented retrieval cutoff of 20 September 2026; there is no lower date cutoff. E6 is deferred during title-and-abstract screening. An English abstract does not establish full-text availability. Under the later high-precision policy, surveys are retained as background or discovery material rather than advanced as primary systems; analytical infrastructure without an explicit analytical workflow is deferred or excluded according to the available evidence. Missing evidence remains distinct from a demonstrated scientific failure.

E7 records sufficiency of evidence for E1--E5, not another system characteristic. A defensible No on any scientific criterion yields Excluded even if other criteria are uncertain. All five Yes responses with sufficient evidence yield provisional Include/Advance; otherwise a non-excluded record is Uncertain/Defer. Invalid responses and interrupted requests are technical states, not exclusions. General frameworks and non-life-science interaction studies may inform the discussion without entering eligible-system counts.

\subsection{Sources, Search Strategy, and Retrieval}
\label{sec:methodology-search}
Identification combined PubMed, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, a local arXiv snapshot, and prior-survey materials. Five overlapping query families addressed relational visualization, assisted visual analysis, interactive systems, nondesktop environments, and conversational analysis. Each combined life-science expressions with relevant system, relationship, assistance, or environment terminology. Assistance-neutral queries were retained because computational contributions are not always advertised as AI. Provider syntax, indexing, and searched fields differ; equivalent conceptual families do not imply equivalent result sets. Query definitions, records, and acquisition checkpoints were retained for the supplementary search record.

Repeated arXiv API failures led to an explicitly registered snapshot substitution. A single pass over version 303's 3,164,528 records yielded 958 distinct matches and 1,333 record/query-family memberships. Matching used Unicode normalization, case folding, whole tokens, contiguous within-field phrases, and explicit Boolean grouping without stemming or fuzzy matching. Equivalence to the API was not established. Observed metadata updates extended through 11 September 2026, which does not prove exhaustive coverage through the overall cutoff.

Prior-survey routes included Ehlers-associated working materials (816 occurrences), Joos-associated companion material (146), and a reconstruction from Fonnet and Pri\'e's bibliography (207).~\cite{Ehlers2025,Joos2025VisNetIA,Fonnet2019ImmersiveAnalytics} The last contained 132 supported survey-member labels, 43 unconfirmed entries, and 32 background references. Working-ledger inclusion was not treated as confirmed survey membership or eligibility. These imports do not establish that recursive citation searching was performed.

\subsection{Occurrence Preservation and Deduplication}
\label{sec:methodology-deduplication}
The registered merge consolidated 187,446 source occurrences into 140,959 deduplicated bibliographic records. A source occurrence is an entry returned by a provider or imported from a retained source; multiple occurrences can refer to the same publication. We use \emph{canonical record} for the representative bibliographic entry retained after applying the identity rules below. This count describes the search output after deduplication, not papers read in full or included in the synthesis. Normalized DOI keys were used where available and exact normalized-title keys otherwise, without fuzzy title matching. A DOI-bearing record did not automatically merge with a DOI-missing record solely because titles agreed. Approved arXiv identity adjudications reduced the generic total from 141,383 by 424 groups; historical decisions and occurrence provenance were retained.

A separate identity overlay confirmed 30 prior-survey groups covering 71 occurrences without changing their canonical membership, survey labels, or scientific eligibility. Fifty related-version links were retained without automatic collapse. A preprint and its published article can therefore remain separate bibliographic records, and multiple papers can describe the same software system. During examination of the selected papers in full, we link related publication versions and group descriptions of the same system so that additional papers do not automatically count as additional systems.

\subsection{Screening, Follow-up, and Human Decisions}
\label{sec:methodology-screening}
The development pilot comprised 70 uniformly selected records, 30 records from five predefined metadata/provenance challenge strata, and ten disjoint calibration records. Optional secondary assignments covered two nonoverlapping subsets of 25 evaluation records. Assignments do not establish completed independent review. Prior model exposure and protocol-development use prevent interpreting the pilot as an independent blinded accuracy benchmark; random and challenge outcomes are not pooled into a prevalence estimate.

The primary pilot was followed by bounded evidence recovery: 18 abstracts were recovered among 31 metadata cases, 13 remained unresolved, and one mixed-provider title/abstract identity was reconciled for separate re-review. A 19-record follow-up preserved the earlier screening judgments alongside the updated assessments. Recovery retrieved existing evidence rather than generating abstracts. A subsequent fast-track policy deferred unresolved cases instead of requiring an open-ended recovery or full-text campaign. This policy favors feasible, explicitly supported candidates and may miss eligible systems with incomplete descriptions; deferral is not scientific exclusion.

The screening campaigns used the OpenAI Responses API with the recorded model identifier \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna}, medium reasoning, default service tier, and no inference-time browsing or tools. Structured responses were checked against supplied evidence identifiers, and outcomes/routes were recomputed locally. Original responses, retries, failures, and prompt/protocol versions were retained. Evidence-format and queue-handoff repairs changed validation or execution mechanics without converting failed requests into judgments. Technical validity does not establish screening accuracy.

Figure~\ref{fig:study-selection-flow} reconciles the effective, supersession-aware screening states. Of 140,959 deduplicated bibliographic records, 140,810 have valid machine-assisted outcomes: 9,505 Advance, 83,258 Defer, and 48,047 Excluded. The remainder comprises 110 protected pilot/calibration records, 35 technical failures, and four ambiguous requests that were not resubmitted; no other records remain unprocessed. Of the 48,047 excluded records, 10,460 were also flagged as background literature: potentially useful for context or discovery, but not advanced as primary system reports. All 9,505 advanced records received descriptive coding. These are versioned model-assisted dispositions, not independently adjudicated final inclusions.

Examination of the papers in full informed these comparisons, while broader category assignments remained provisional. Anchors were purposefully selected to support comparative synthesis rather than to estimate prevalence.

\subsection{Multilabel Coding and Synthesis}
\label{sec:methodology-synthesis}
The tasks, assistance modes, and modalities are defined in Section~\ref{sec:foundations}. Labels are independently Present, Absent, or Uncertain and may co-occur. Broad claims of intelligence or exploration do not establish every label; missing modality information does not imply a desktop environment. Locus of analytic agency is interpretive, not an additional eligibility score or category axis.

The machine-coding campaign assigned the frozen categories to all 9,505 candidate records using their supplied title/abstract evidence. Those assignments organize discovery and synthesis; they are not population estimates or category frequencies verified by examining the papers in full. Detailed comparisons use passages from the selected papers about inputs, mechanisms, actions, human intervention, and evaluation. Paper-level label combinations do not imply that every mechanism supports every task or modality. Related versions are linked, and implemented capabilities, measured outcomes, and proposed extensions are distinguished.

This design has deliberate limits. Missing metadata and high-precision advancement can bias the candidate set; accessible and well-described systems are easier to assess. Searches and snapshot substitution do not establish exhaustive coverage. The development pilot is not an independent reference standard, contextual studies have different scopes, and heterogeneous evaluations do not support a pooled effect estimate. The report therefore offers a structured, evidence-bounded synthesis, not a claim that every candidate was independently screened or that all eligible software has been enumerated. The processing counts describe candidate identification and screening; they do not establish a closed, fully human-assessed inclusion set.


For the mechanism-level map (Figure~\ref{fig:assistance-modality-map}), purposeful assessment prioritized sparse combinations and contrasting mechanisms. Across six assessment waves, 384 selected publication entries represent 381 systems (Figure~\ref{fig:study-selection-flow}). Exact candidate-pool links exist for 376 publication entries and 373 systems; eight systems entered through additional discovery routes. The complete text was accessible for 135 publication entries; one entry provided only an abstract, and the full text was unavailable for 248 entries. Three accessible entries describe related publications about systems already represented in the assessment: 132 systems received completed assessments, while 249 did not. Of 80 eligible systems, 76 support 118 system--cell placements; four lack a supported placement in the assessed map. Nineteen systems remain scientifically unresolved and 33 are excluded or contextual. System grouping follows the retained identity crosswalk; unresolved same-title pairs were not silently merged. A targeted pass examined all 144 candidate memberships in Immersive--Desktop/Planar, Immersive--Large Display, and Adaptive--Large Display, resolving to 131 records and 129 system groups. Of 142 deduplicated system--cell cases, 30 were assessed using the complete papers: 28 unsupported and two unresolved; 112 were access-limited. No new placement was established. Automated assessments remain subject to author adjudication, not independent double coding. The matrix preserves identities, report versions, locators, and qualifications; selection and access constraints preclude prevalence estimates.


A separate offline census summarizes the 9,505 distinct candidate bibliographic records using the frozen machine-coding contract. Each assistance, modality, and task label has a PRESENT, ABSENT, or UNCERTAIN state. The export stores PRESENT and UNCERTAIN lists; omission denotes ABSENT under this exhaustive contract. Table~\ref{tab:candidate-census} counts records with both labels PRESENT. These co-occurrences guide discovery and do not establish that the assistance operates in the named environment. For example, Desktop/Planar is PRESENT in 2,912 records but UNCERTAIN in 4,072. Missing specificity in metadata and the preceding high-precision selection prevent interpretation as the distribution of the wider literature. The candidate-record denominator must also remain distinct from the system-level assessment set in Figure~\ref{fig:assistance-modality-map}.

\subsection{Use of generative AI} 
\label{sec:ai-disclosure}
OpenAI ChatGPT and Codex assisted with development of the literature-processing software, evidence extraction and organization, comparative synthesis, manuscript drafting and revision, and preparation of LaTeX, tables, and figures. Automated title-and-abstract screening and provisional category coding used the OpenAI Responses API with the recorded model identifier `gpt-5.6-luna`, as described above. Google Gemini also contributed preliminary calibration judgments during protocol development; these were not treated as independent human reviews. Validation included software tests, checks of record identities and source passages, deterministic recomputation of screening outcomes, citation checks, and inspection of rendered figures and documents. These checks address technical consistency and traceability; they do not constitute independent human validation of the entire candidate pool. AI-assisted eligibility and category assessments remain distinguished from author-adjudicated decisions. Reproduced interface images were extracted or cropped from their cited sources rather than generated synthetically. The lead author reviewed all AI-generated content incorporated into the manuscript. The authors retain responsibility for the interpretation, accuracy, attribution, and final content of the paper.
