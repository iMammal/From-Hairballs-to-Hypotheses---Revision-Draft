\section{Review Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The review examines computational assistance within life-science visual analysis through a common protocol for eligibility, record reconciliation, screening, and synthesis. Eligibility establishes whether a paper reports a qualifying system. The task, assistance, and modality perspectives in Section~\ref{sec:foundations} then describe its contribution, while the locus of analytic agency guides interpretation of computational interventions and human responsibilities. Neither classification nor synthesis priority determines eligibility.

The eligibility rules follow the frozen rubric, version 1.0.0, dated 30 August 2026. The subsections below distinguish these rules and documented implementation from procedures whose execution remains incomplete. Final retrieval counts, validation results, and artifact availability will be reported after reconciliation.

\subsection{Review Scope and Eligibility}
\label{sec:methodology-eligibility}

The screening unit is a paper. At least one identified system within it must jointly satisfy E1--E7; evidence for unrelated systems cannot be combined to establish eligibility. Systems and mechanisms provide units for comparison but are not additional corpus members.

\paragraph{E1---Life-science application.} The system must support analysis, understanding, monitoring, decision-making, discovery, or scientific practice in a life-science or closely related domain, including biomedical and clinical science, health, neuroscience, ecology, marine science, biology, and bioinformatics. Clinical and surgical systems follow the same rules: training, teleoperation, procedural guidance, or presentation alone does not establish an analytic workflow.

\paragraph{E2---Relational, derived-structure, or multiscale relevance.} The system must analyze explicit networks or relationships, or relationships derived from spatial, temporal, multivariate, image-derived, lineage, similarity, or other multiscale life-science data. A node--link representation is not required, but the relationships must have an analytic role.

\paragraph{E3---Visual-analytics component.} Humans must use an interactive or analytically meaningful visual representation to inspect, understand, validate, steer, compare, interpret, or act on data or computational results. Static illustration, presentation, communication, or artistic output alone does not qualify.

\paragraph{E4---Substantive computational assistance.} A computational mechanism must contribute nontrivially within the interactive visual-analytics workflow. It may organize or transform data, adapt interaction, recommend or initiate actions, mediate explanations, or exploit immersive properties as assistance. Qualifying mechanisms include layout, clustering, dimensionality reduction, graph computation, aggregation, annotation, ranking, prediction, natural-language analytic operations, and orchestration. Ordinary rendering, pan, zoom, click, literal manual selection, direct parameter entry, and ordinary database lookup alone do not qualify. An AI or agentic label does not establish the mechanism's behavior.

\paragraph{E5---Human analytic relationship.} Humans must meaningfully inspect, direct, validate, interpret, collaborate with, supervise, correct, or make decisions from the assisted process. Fully offline or automated computation without this relationship is excluded; manual control over every operation is not required.

\paragraph{E6---Administrative scope.} Eligible documents are journal, conference, workshop, and preprint research papers with English-language full text available for review. There is no lower publication-date cutoff; the upper boundary is the documented retrieval end date. Surveys and conceptual papers qualify only when they independently report or analyze a system satisfying E1--E5. Publication community is not an additional restriction.

\paragraph{E7---Evidence sufficiency.} Reviewed evidence must identify a candidate system and support defensible decisions for E1--E6. Missing title, abstract, or metadata detail does not establish ineligibility. Exclusion for insufficient evidence requires documented escalation and human review where required; retrieval or parsing failure alone is insufficient.

Each criterion receives Yes, No, or Uncertain, with supporting evidence. Final eligibility requires seven Yes decisions; a conclusive No establishes exclusion, and unresolved criteria without a No leave eligibility uncertain. These rules apply across sources, tasks, assistance modes, and environments. The provisional title-and-abstract outcome described in Section~\ref{sec:methodology-screening} is distinct from final eligibility. Prior corpus membership and historical relevance scores confer no exemption. Noneligible surveys, frameworks, and transferable studies may inform background or retrieval but remain outside corpus counts.

\subsection{Sources, Search Strategy, and Retrieval}
\label{sec:methodology-sources}

The search combines bibliographic sources with candidates from prior surveys. The frozen external-search plan covers PubMed, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar, arXiv, IEEE Xplore, and the ACM Digital Library, providing biomedical, multidisciplinary, preprint, and computing routes into the literature. These routes overlap and may describe the same system through its scientific application, computation, interface, or environment. Their source occurrences remain distinguishable during reconciliation.

Five overlapping query families combine life-science terms with the following concepts:
\begin{description}
\item[QF01: Relational visualization.] Visualization and relational or multiscale terms, without requiring assistance terminology.
\item[QF02: Assisted visual analysis.] Visualization or interactive-system terms combined with assistance terms.
\item[QF03: Interactive systems.] Interactive-system or specific visual-analytics terms combined with the revised relational expression, accommodating tools, platforms, explorers, and workbenches.
\item[QF04: Nondesktop environments.] Environment terms combined with visualization, interactive-system, or exploration terms.
\item[QF05: Conversational analysis.] Conversational terms combined with visualization, interactive-system, or analysis terms.
\end{description}
These are retrieval routes rather than coding categories. Assistance-neutral queries admit systems whose computational contribution is not advertised as AI; matching assistance or environment vocabulary does not itself establish eligibility.

Search reporting will specify full executed expressions, fields, dates, filters, routes, and completion evidence, using PRISMA-S as a reporting reference~\cite{MethodsRethlefsen2021}. A conceptual query does not guarantee identical phrase matching or field coverage across providers. The implementation supports paginated API retrieval and ACM export import. Export partitions, interrupted requests, quota pauses, and changing result totals require reconciliation before a route is considered complete. Any local metadata-snapshot route must identify its snapshot, temporal coverage, and matching policy separately: the arXiv snapshot audit established local matching behavior, not equivalence to the API.

Seed materials derive from Ehlers et al.'s biological-network survey~\cite{Ehlers2025}, Joos et al.'s immersive network-analysis survey~\cite{Joos2025VisNetIA}, and Fonnet and Pri\'e's immersive-analytics survey~\cite{Fonnet2019ImmersiveAnalytics}. Companion resources, working ledgers, and bibliographies differ in whether they establish survey membership or merely identify candidates. Seed records retain those distinctions and source locations. Survey membership does not confer eligibility, and reference extraction does not establish that recursive backward or forward citation searching occurred. Supplementary documentation will identify the artifacts and any additional citation-searching procedure actually executed. Final source yields, seed reconciliation, and the global cutoff remain pending.

\subsection{Occurrence Preservation and Deduplication}
\label{sec:methodology-deduplication}

A paper may appear in several sources or query families. The pipeline preserves each occurrence while grouping records that refer to the same bibliographic identity. Database occurrences retain their source, query, retrieval run, and available native identifiers; imported records retain artifact and entry locations. A canonical record enumerates its constituent occurrences. Each occurrence has one effective duplicate decision and belongs to one canonical group, while conflicting source metadata remain inspectable.

Generic matching uses a normalized DOI when available and an exact normalized-title key otherwise. Title fallback is deterministic, not a similarity search. A DOI-bearing record and a DOI-missing record with the same title can therefore receive different keys and require subsequent identity adjudication. The reproducibility record must specify the normalization functions and matching-rule versions used. Source-specific metadata remain available when records disagree. For example, a title-only survey record and an arXiv snapshot record may initially share a title group, while another database supplies a DOI-bearing record for the same paper. An approved identity decision can consolidate these occurrences without discarding their separate retrieval histories. A relationship between records must therefore be supported by the identity decision, rather than inferred merely from their position in an imported ledger.

Approved adjudications supplement this grouping. Snapshot integration distinguishes direct decisions about arXiv occurrences from propagation to compatible records already grouped under a title key. Regrouping checks contradictory identifiers, competing targets, unresolved restrictions, and cyclic transfers before consolidating occurrences. Earlier decisions remain linked to their replacements. Uncertain identities inherited from survey materials retain their unresolved status and source-group references; canonical grouping does not resolve conflicting evidence by itself.

Related-version links are separate from duplicate decisions. A preprint and published article may be related without being interchangeable, and the link alone does not merge them. Likewise, multiple reports of one system are not necessarily independent studies. Any grouping of reports for synthesis must be documented separately, with the relevant counting unit. Identity uncertainty and eligibility uncertainty also remain distinct.

The merge uses designated source datasets and checks occurrence preservation. Superseded checkpoints remain acquisition history rather than additional inputs. Flow reporting will distinguish source occurrences, duplicate consolidation, resulting canonical records, and unresolved identities. Deduplication is not an eligibility exclusion; final counts must be traceable through effective decisions to the records screened.

\subsection{Screening, Full-Text Escalation, and Human Decisions}
\label{sec:methodology-screening}

Title-and-abstract screening assesses the reported system against the eligibility criteria, recording criterion values, evidence locations, and concise rationales. The current validation packet defers E6 administrative checks and assesses E7 as evidence sufficiency for E1--E5 at this stage. Its INCLUDE outcome means advancement to further assessment, not final eligibility. Retained records and unresolved scientific assessments advance to full-text review; final membership still requires E1--E7. This stage-specific interpretation requires a documented amendment to the frozen rubric.

LLM outputs are proposed decisions, not authoritative corpus membership. The record preserves reviewed inputs, rubric version, model and prompt provenance, proposed values, supporting evidence, rationale, and technical status. Administrative eligibility requires publication and full-text evidence, rather than inferred topical relevance. Malformed responses and failed inference attempts are recorded as technical failures.

Full-text assessment resolves omissions, conflicting descriptions, and distinctions that abstracts may not establish, including substantive assistance versus ordinary interaction, immersive assistance versus immersive display, and analysis versus presentation or procedural guidance. An exclusion based only on a missing or incomplete abstract requires escalation. The earlier rubric requires escalation for any uncertain criterion, whereas the current packet normally does not escalate records with a conclusive exclusion. The precedence of these rules remains to be reconciled before production screening is described as finalized.

Acquisition attempts and outcomes remain part of the record. Retrieval or parsing failure leaves the scientific decision unresolved while further acquisition or review remains possible. Insufficient-evidence exclusion requires documented escalation and the prescribed human review. Confirmed administrative ineligibility is recorded under its own criterion; an inaccessible PDF is not evidence that a paper is non-English.

Exclusions retain a primary reason and supported secondary reasons. Precedence follows the rubric: date, language availability, document type, life-science application, relational relevance, visual-analytics component, substantive assistance, human analytic relationship, then insufficient evidence after escalation. Other protocol-based reasons require a versioned explanation. Duplicates, missing abstracts, technical failures, and historical relevance labels are not scientific exclusion reasons.

A human decision supersedes the corresponding machine proposal for the criterion reviewed, retaining the earlier proposal in the history. Disagreements between independent human judgments, where obtained, require recorded adjudication; this does not imply universal double screening. The final report must identify actual decision-makers, review coverage, and access to machine proposals. Authors remain responsible for synthesis. The final account must distinguish the records and criteria reviewed by humans from those supported only by machine proposals. It must also identify whether reviewers saw earlier judgments. A provision for adjudicating disagreement does not establish that independent judgments were collected, and a populated validation workbook does not by itself establish reviewer independence or the origin of its annotations.

The frozen protocol reserves final validation design until after a bounded pilot and before validation results are examined. Reporting must specify the actual sampling frame and selection, reviewer roles, available information, disagreement and missing-review handling, and planned measures. The current 100-record re-analysis packet contains judgments and rationales but does not establish independent panel agreement or corpus-wide screening validity. Criterion-level and aggregate agreement, pre-adjudication results, and subsequent corrections require separate reporting after validation is reconciled.

\subsection{Multilabel Coding and Synthesis}
\label{sec:methodology-coding}

Eligible systems receive independent Present, Absent, or Uncertain annotations for the task, assistance, and modality categories defined in Section~\ref{sec:foundations}. Multiple labels are permitted without a primary-category tie-break. A general claim of exploration does not establish all five tasks, and insufficient evidence does not justify a default assistance mode. Immersive display alone does not establish Immersive assistance. Modality labels require analytic visualization in the stated environment, rather than possible deployment or use of a device only as a controller. The manuscript's Desktop/Planar label corresponds to the rubric's Planar 2D category, stored as Desktop 2D.

Labels link to supporting passages and source locations. For Present or Uncertain assistance annotations, the rubric requires the identified system and mechanism, input or signal, computational behavior, output or action, human role or control, and analytic consequence. Task and modality annotations require evidence of the supported action and environment. Capability evidence is distinguished from evidence of benefit, which is assessed in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}. A passage describing a recommendation can establish the mechanism without showing that it improves reasoning. Similarly, a task label requires evidence of the intended action, not simply the presence of a relevant interface element. Conflicting or incomplete descriptions remain visible in the annotations so that the synthesis can distinguish a documented absence from an unresolved capability.

The comparison unit is a documented mechanism within its qualifying system and task context. When systems combine mechanisms, their contributions are distinguished where evidence permits and linked to the shared workflow. Paper-level assistance labels are the union of supported modes across qualifying systems; this does not imply every task--mode--modality combination. Tables retain these associations, and frequency summaries must state their counting unit and overlapping labels. Reuse of a mechanism across comparisons does not create independent papers or evaluations.

Synthesis priority follows eligibility. Core papers centrally document the qualifying system and assistance relationship; Supporting papers provide narrower or secondary evidence relevant to a synthesis question; Contextual papers supply in-scope historical, framing, evaluation, or adjacent-system context. These priorities guide discussion depth and placement, not membership or study quality. Any quantitative subset or narrative selection requires its own stated basis.

The locus of analytic agency guides interpretation of who proposes, scopes, executes, and assesses an operation, and how analysts can inspect, revise, or reverse it. These responsibilities are inferred from documented workflows rather than assigned another categorical score. Two systems may both support Comparison and Differentiation through Algorithmic assistance while differing in whether scientists select the candidates or the system recommends them. The comparison retains that difference in initiative alongside the common task and mode. It does not infer scientific authority from automated execution or from a confirmation step alone. Synthesis connects the coded dimensions to evaluation evidence while distinguishing reported findings from the review's interpretations and research proposals.

\subsection{Review Flow, Reproducibility, and Limitations}
\label{sec:methodology-limitations}

The final flow diagram will reconcile identification, duplicate consolidation, screening, full-text acquisition and assessment, exclusions with reasons, and the eligible corpus, using PRISMA 2020 as a reporting reference~\cite{MethodsPage2021}. Source occurrences and canonical papers require separate counts; unresolved identities and assessments remain visible until disposition. Historical corpus totals cannot substitute for the regenerated record. Flow counts and validation results remain pending, and no completed PRISMA-compliance claim is made here.

The intended supplementary record comprises versioned protocols and amendments; executed queries, dates, and source and seed provenance; matching rules; screening and coding instructions; model and prompt specifications; human decisions; and count derivations. Stable identifiers connect records across stages. The final availability statement must identify released artifacts, versions, persistent locations, and access restrictions. Internal retention is not public availability, and code alone cannot reproduce changing provider indexes or nondeterministic model responses. The retained record must therefore include the evidence actually reviewed and the outputs that informed decisions, not only instructions for regenerating them. Full search strings, source-specific matching definitions, acquisition logs, technical settings, and detailed adjudication histories belong in the supplementary record; the main text reports the decisions needed to understand the corpus boundary and the synthesis. Versioned amendments must distinguish changes to the procedure from corrections made while applying it.

Coverage is limited by terminology, indexed fields, provider coverage, unavailable abstracts or full texts, language restrictions, and differences between API and snapshot matching. Survey materials can also leave membership or identity unresolved. Overlapping sources broaden retrieval without establishing exhaustiveness.

Screening and synthesis introduce judgment uncertainty: models may infer unsupported mechanisms or human roles, and reviewers may disagree on boundary cases. Evidence passages and decision histories make these judgments inspectable without eliminating error. Validation findings apply according to the sample design and cannot automatically be generalized to the corpus. These review-process limitations are distinct from limitations of included studies, examined in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.
