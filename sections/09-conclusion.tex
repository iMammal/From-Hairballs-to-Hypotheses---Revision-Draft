\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Systems supporting related life-science tasks differ in the computational work they perform and the decisions scientists retain. The comparisons distinguish configuring a pipeline from teaching a model through labeled examples, organizing a representation from optimizing its layout, and interrogating predictions from assessing evidence for an interpretation. These differences call for evaluation of the particular contribution: correct execution, responsive classification, appropriate representation, faithful translation of intent, or a defensible evidential basis. Scientists' ability to inspect, correct, and interpret the results remains consequential across these arrangements.

The Assistance $\times$ Visualization Modality framework organizes these mechanisms and environments, while the task groups and locus of analytic agency connect them to analytical purposes and decisions. This organization aims to help researchers compare design alternatives and identify evaluation requirements appropriate to the work supported or delegated. The resulting agenda emphasizes revisable delegation, inspectable transformations, traceable evidence, collaborative common ground, and continuity of analytical context across environments. Progress from hairballs to hypotheses is meaningful when scientists can explain what an assisted analysis suggests and why its evidence warrants the next question.
