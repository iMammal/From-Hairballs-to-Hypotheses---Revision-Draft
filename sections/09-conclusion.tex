\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Systems supporting related life-science tasks differ in the computational work they perform and the decisions scientists retain. Comparing them across analytic tasks reveals distinctions including configuring a pipeline versus teaching a model through labeled examples, organizing a representation versus optimizing its layout, and interrogating predictions versus assessing evidence for an interpretation. These comparisons identify different evaluation targets: pipeline execution and label propagation, representational changes and layout quality, or the evidence supporting an interpretation. Across all these arrangements, what matters is whether scientists can inspect, correct, and interpret the results.

The Assistance $\times$ Visualization Modality framework organizes these mechanisms and environments, while the task groups and locus of analytic agency connect them to analytical purposes and decisions. This organization aims to help researchers compare design alternatives and identify evaluation requirements appropriate to the work supported or delegated. The resulting agenda emphasizes revisable delegation, inspectable transformations, traceable evidence, collaborative common ground, and continuity of analytical context across environments. Progress from hairballs to hypotheses is meaningful when scientists can explain what an assisted analysis suggests and why its evidence warrants the next question.
