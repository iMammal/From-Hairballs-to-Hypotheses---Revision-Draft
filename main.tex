\begin{abstract}
Life-science visual analytics supports scientists in exploring biological data, interpreting patterns, and developing hypotheses. Displaying dense biological relationships creates visual ``hairballs'' when overlapping connections obscure individual relationships and broader patterns. Systems supporting related scientific tasks differ in what computation contributes and which decisions remain with scientists. We present an Assistance $\times$ Visualization Modality framework that connects these differences to analytical work and its evaluation. The framework organizes computational mechanisms and display environments around five task groups: navigation, selection, comparison, sensemaking, and collaboration. We examine the locus of analytic agency, here understood as how initiative and control over analytical actions are distributed between scientists and computation, and its implications for interpreting and validating results.

We screened 140,810 deduplicated bibliographic records using their titles and available abstracts, identifying 9,505 candidates for further assessment. We selected systems to compare different computational contributions to related analytical tasks and examined their full-text reports. For each system, we assessed eligibility, framework placement, computational mechanisms, and evaluation methods. The comparisons distinguish configuring an analysis pipeline from teaching a model, organizing a visual representation from optimizing it, and interrogating predictions from assessing supporting evidence. These differences identify distinct evaluation requirements for computational outputs, scientists' ability to inspect and correct them, and the interpretations they support.

The framework aims to help researchers compare design alternatives and identify evaluation methods appropriate to the analytical decisions supported or delegated. The resulting research priorities include revisable delegation, inspectable transformations, traceable evidence, and continuity of analytical context across collaborators and display environments.

\end{abstract}
