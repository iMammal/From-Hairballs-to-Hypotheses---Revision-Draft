\begin{abstract}
Life-science analysis increasingly combines computational assistance with interactive visual representations of relational and multiscale data. However, the presence of artificial intelligence or an immersive interface does not establish how a system supports scientific reasoning. This report organizes the design space through an Assistance $\times$ Visualization Modality framework, using five analytic task groups as a comparative lens and the locus of analytic agency to examine consequential choices. A staged literature-screening workflow identified 9,505 candidate publications for further assessment and provisionally categorized them by assistance mode, visualization modality, and analytical task. A purposefully selected set of papers was then examined in full to support a focused comparative synthesis. Comparisons distinguish pipeline configuration from model teaching, representation organization from optimization, and prediction interrogation from evidence assessment. Evaluation is examined in terms of effectiveness, fidelity, provenance, control, workload, and collaboration. The resulting agenda emphasizes revisable delegation, inspectable transformations, evidence-linked generation, collaborative common ground, and continuity across environments. Together, the framework and synthesis connect computational mechanisms to the scientific work they enable and the evidence needed to establish their value.
\end{abstract}
